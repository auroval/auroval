# nightshade-gen

Generates a nightshade scene candidate from a brief that records only what
varies between scenes.

```nu
python3 tools/nightshade-gen/generate.py tools/nightshade-gen/scene-02-shrine.json ~/Pictures/auroval-nightshade/scene-02 --dry-run
python3 tools/nightshade-gen/generate.py tools/nightshade-gen/scene-02-shrine.json ~/Pictures/auroval-nightshade/scene-02 --seeds 1,3,7
```

Masters are **assets and are not in this repository** — the same arrangement
`nightshade-loop` uses. What is committed is the brief and the code that turns
it into a prompt.

## Why this exists

scene-01 was generated by a script in a scratch directory, and the script was
thrown away. Its `source.md` says *"everything needed to regenerate this image
byte-for-byte is below"* and records the style and subject clauses; the other
seven — §4.1's blockquote and §4.2's bullets — were assembled at runtime by
that script. **scene-01's full prompt cannot be recovered.** The image is a
one-off, and the set it was supposed to begin cannot be extended in its voice,
which is the exact failure `nightshade-assets.md` §3 says `source.md` is there
to prevent.

The fix is not a better `source.md`. It is that the assembly is code, in the
repository, and every run writes the string it actually used.

## Two surfaces, and why they are not the same picture

A brief must say `surface`: `greeter` or `desktop`. There is no default,
because the two want nearly opposite framings and a brief that does not say
which it is for was written without the distinction in mind.

`nightshade-assets.md` §1 is explicit about what a scene is: *"A scene is not
a wallpaper; it is the thing a clock, a date, a username and a password field
sit on top of."* Almost all of §4.1 and the whole of §4.2 follow from that --
they exist to keep a rectangle clear for UI that a wallpaper does not have.

Applied to a wallpaper those clauses are not merely unnecessary, they are
wrong. "Near-black field below and around the subject" and "the lower middle
of the frame is empty near-black space" produce a frame that is two-thirds
empty, which on a desktop reads as a login screen missing its login form --
because that is exactly what it is.

So the desktop set keeps what nightshade *is* -- the rendering style, neon rim
light and glow, deep darkness, grain -- and replaces "against darkness" with
"in darkness": the dark sits between the forms instead of instead of them, and
the frame is filled edge to edge.

This field exists because of a mistake. Six scene-02 candidates were generated
under the greeter's clauses when what was wanted was a wallpaper, and nothing
in the brief or the tool could have caught it.

## What is in here rather than in the brief

| in `generate.py` | why |
|---|---|
| the style clause | §4.1.1: a theme has exactly one rendering style. A brief that sets `style` is **refused**, not merged — "two scenes drawn in different styles do not read as one theme", and a per-scene override would quietly undo the section. |
| §4.1's six constant clauses | They are the theme, not the scene. |
| §4.2's four constraints | Restated as positive assertions, for the reason below. |
| model, revision, steps, guidance, size | A candidate that differs in subject *and* settings tells you nothing about either. |

The brief holds `tag` and `subject`. That is all a scene is.

## The negations are inverted, and this is the subtle part

§4.2's bullets are all "no X". None of them reaches the model that way.

FLUX.2-klein is distilled and runs at `guidance_scale=1.0`, so there is no
classifier-free guidance and therefore **no negative prompt**. A "no X" in the
positive prompt supplies the tokens for X with nothing to oppose them: "no
glowing lamp" asks for a glowing lamp. Each bullet is turned into a statement
of what the frame contains instead — "the centre of the frame is unlit and
empty, the brightest areas are confined to the top of the image".

This applies to the **subject** clause too, and that is easy to forget because
the subject feels like prose rather than configuration. scene-02's first draft
ended "no ground, no path, no lantern"; it would have painted a lantern.

## Requirements

`requirements.txt`, into a venv. The weights are pinned by revision hash and
`diffusers` by exact version, which closes scene-01's first recorded gap —
pinned weights beside unpinned code make "reproducible a year later" only half
true.

`diffusers==0.42.1` is the release that carries `Flux2KleinPipeline`.
scene-01 predates it and used `0.42.0.dev0` from git main, so a version pin
was not available then and a commit hash would have been the best possible
record; it is now, and this is the stronger one.

`torch` is deliberately left unpinned, and `requirements.txt` says why: its
CUDA build must match the installed driver, and it is not what decides how the
image looks. The resolved version goes into every `run.json`.

Needs a CUDA GPU. `enable_model_cpu_offload()` rather than `.to("cuda")`: a 4B
transformer and its text encoder at 2560×1440 do not fit a consumer card
alongside a running compositor, and offloading costs wall-clock and nothing
else.

## What this tool does not do

It does not judge. `tools/scene-check/scene_check.py` measures a candidate
against the contract, and gates 2 and 3 — an hour behind the real greeter, and
two weeks of living with it — are not measurements at all.
